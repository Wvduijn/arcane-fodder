## Verdict: FAIL (one major, narrow fix)

The trail itself is good. Crumbs land at exact path-length spacing across frame rates, long frames
interpolate, zero-length moves add nothing, trimming keeps exactly one crumb at or past the keep
length, and slots move continuously while the head walks. The module is pure and immutable. The
problem is `rebaseTo`, the succession half. When the leader's trail folds back on itself, which
happens on every reversal, it keeps part of the fold. Followers' slots then jump forward by up to
about 15 px at the moment of succession. That breaks D3's "the trail compacts without snapping", and
it breaks this file's own test claim "never moves a slot further than the new leader was from its old
slot". T6 and T8 build on this, so it's cheaper to fix now.

## Findings

### Blocker
None.

### Major

**M1. `rebaseTo` snaps slots when the trail folds back on itself.** (owner: core-dev; D3)
src/core/squad/trail.ts:106-111 and :125-139. `firstCrumbBehindNearest` searches the whole polyline
for the point nearest the new leader and breaks ties toward the head (`d < best`). After a reversal,
the same spot lies on the trail twice: at arc 6 and at arc 26 in the probe. The tie goes to arc 6, so
the dropped part is too short, the 12 px fold (44 → 50 → 44) survives, and every slot behind the new
leader shifts forward by that amount at once. With a few px of separation offset the jump grows to
15 px. The followers still walk rather than teleport, but the trail and the targets snap. That's
visible in the overlay and makes followers 2 and 3 lurch at the exact moment D3 says nothing should
jump. Reversals are routine: stop-starts, zig-zags and backing off from enemies all do it.
The test at trail.test.ts:237-245 ("picks the trail point nearest the head on a tie") encodes this
behaviour.

Fix (recommended): rebase by arc distance, not by nearest point. The caller knows where the new
leader's slot was: k × `followerSpacingPx`, with k = 1 for an ordinary succession, or the lowest
surviving index for same-frame deaths. So:
`rebaseTo(trail, point, slotArcPx)` → cut the trail at `pointAtDistance(trail, slotArcPx)`, keep
everything behind the cut unchanged, and join `point` to the cut with a straight piece (its length is
the new leader's slot error). This is exact when the new leader is on its slot, whatever the path
shape. It also bounds every slot jump by the new leader's own slot error, which is the guarantee the
test at :226 claims. If you'd rather keep the nearest-point search, restrict it to a window of about
`slotArcPx ± followerSpacingPx` and break ties by `|arc − slotArcPx|`.
Tests to add:
1. 20 px reversal, leader dies with follower 1 on its slot: slot jumps are 0 and the length drops by
   exactly 26.
2. The same with a 3 px nudge: the jump is ≤ the nudge.
3. A two-leaders-dead rebase (`slotArcPx` = 52) on a folded trail.
4. Replace or reword the tie test at :237 so it no longer asserts the head-side tie wins on a fold.

### Minor
None.

### Nit

**n1. The head segment treats chord length as arc length.** :87 interpolates head → crumb 0 linearly
over `headArcPx`, while the actual walked arc may bend across several frames. The error is below one
crumb spacing (< 4 px) and continuous. Documented in your design note, so no action needed.
