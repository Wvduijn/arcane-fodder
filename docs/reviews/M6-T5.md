### Minor

**m1. A strike can pick a target an earlier blast killed on the same frame.** (owner: core-dev)
`pickCentre` picks from `input.targets` as passed at the start of the frame (documented), and all
of a frame's blasts are computed before the caller resolves any of them. At 600 and 1200 ms
Firestorm's meteorlet and bolt land on the same frame by design. At 30 fps (33 ms frames) a
Thunderstorm also never has two bolts in one frame, but a hitch frame can. So the bolt may pick a
goblin the meteorlet just killed and land on its corpse spot, a wasted strike. That breaks no
rule (the picks are still in the ring and the blast still resolves there), but it makes Firestorm
weaker in exactly the showcase moment. Fix options: return blasts lazily (a `nextBlast(isAlive)`
iterator or a callback the caller invokes between resolutions), or accept and document it. If you
accept it, I'll watch the T8 sim's Combust count for Firestorm on a 6-pack.

**m2. `GroundBurnTick` has no `teams`, so the caller picks them.** (owner: core-dev)
Blasts carry `teams: ['enemy']` inside the event, but a burn tick leaves the team list to T10's
`applyAreaStatus` call. Burning on wizards would be a silent bug (they can be Burning: the status
exists for any unit). Fix: add `teams: ['enemy']` to `GroundBurnTick` (or make the tick an
`AreaStatusEvent`), so T10 forwards it as-is like the blasts. In M7, huts are enemy-team: the
"not huts" call means the hut bodies must stay out of `units` for this call, or the tick needs a
unit-only filter. Worth one doc line now.

### Nit

**n1. `ConvergenceBlast` doc doesn't mention `element`.** channel.ts:138 says to emit
`convergence:impact` with combo, centre and radius. The contract now also has `element` (M6-T0
M1). It's `event.blast.element`; add it to the comment (which also brings that line under 100
chars; channel.ts:14 is 103 too).

**n2. GDD benchmark "one meteorlet does 87".** 35 × 2.5 = 87.5 rounds to 88 here (halves up, the
same rule as area.ts). The code is consistent; the GDD §10 benchmark text is approximate. Owner
game-designer, if they want it exact.
