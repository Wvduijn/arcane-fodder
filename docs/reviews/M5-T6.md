## Verdict: PASS WITH NOTES

The reaction code matches GDD §7 and §8 and the sprint's architecture:
- **One place.** Every hit that can react goes through `resolveUnitHit` or
  `resolveAreaWithReactions`, and every blast through `resolveAreaEvent`.
- **Combust.** Consumes Burning on the origin. The blast hits enemies only, the origin included,
  and spreads Burning to everyone hit except the origin. Kegs in range are listed, and credit goes
  to the storm hit's source.
- **Kaboom.** Hits everything in range at ×0.5 on wizards, puts Burning on enemies only, pushes
  every survivor (wizards included), credits the keg and lists the other idle kegs for the cascade.
- **Keg state.** idle → fuse → spent. A second trigger does nothing, physical never lights a keg,
  and autos never reach kegs (they only use `resolveUnitHit`, which lists kegs only through a
  Combust).
- **Fuse timing.** The `beforeStep` rule keeps a chain `fuseMs` apart to within a frame and shows
  the ring for the full fuse.

The tests cover the GDD §7 verify, the §8 verify (wizard at 70 takes 30, at 90 nothing; a chain of 3
at 64 px in order, 300 ms apart, at 30/60/144 fps) and every trigger and non-trigger source.

**Rule call (main asked): Combust is decided on Burning *before* the storm hit. I agree.** GDD §4 says
"Lightning on a Burning target triggers Combust", and GDD §7 says the storm hit "resolves normally"
first, with the origin included in the blast. Lightning (30) one-shots every MVP goblin and death
clears statuses (§6). A post-hit rule would make the §4 line false in every case, and with it D4's
"Lightning on a burning goblin always Combusts". With the pre-hit rule the origin dies electrified,
from the killing blow (§12a rule 1), and the blast goes off at its position. The guards hold: the
hit must have `landed`, the unit must be alive and burning at hit time, and a second storm hit the
same frame finds no Burning. I recommend game-designer adds one line to GDD §7 so the rule is
written down: "A storm hit that kills a burning unit still Combusts at its position."

There are two majors. Neither is a defect in T6's code: both are integration consequences that
another task has to absorb, and they are listed here so they aren't lost.

### Major

**M1. With Combust on, the M4 no-input fight never reaches melee; 21 encounter tests fail.** (owner: core-dev for T8, game-designer for the feel call; D4, M4 D2)
core-dev's in-progress encounterSim (T8) routes every hit through `resolveUnitHit`. In all 9 runs
(3 seeds × 30/60/144 fps), the grunt pack now dies before a single swing lands.
`expect(r.meleeSwings).toBeGreaterThan(0)` fails (encounter.test.ts:47), and so do the "leader
killed by goblins goes through succession" and "1-HP wizards die one by one" cases, because no
wizard is ever hit. Combust is working as designed: two Stormcallers spark about 5×/s, and each
Combust deals 10 to most of a 32 px-spaced pack and re-spreads Burning. This is not a T6 bug, but
it has two consequences:
1. **Tests.** T8 must not delete or loosen these M4 assertions (CLAUDE.md). Options: give the sim
   an explicit `reactions: false` option, used only by the M4 succession and 1-HP cases, each with a
   comment saying why; or keep reactions on and change the setup of those cases (for example spawn
   the pack inside melee range) so they still exercise goblin kills and succession.
   `meleeSwings > 0` with reactions on is a feel question, not a correctness one (point 2).
2. **Feel.** The M4 lesson "Grunt: swarms you" no longer happens in the dev arena with zero input.
   game-designer should decide whether that's acceptable (Combust is meant to be strong) or whether
   to retune: Combust blast damage, spark cadence, or the pack's spacing and distance. Retuning is
   game-designer's data change, not code. I'll report the in-game feel at the gate. The fun verdict
   belongs to the CD.

**M2. A surviving Combust origin keeps its Burning overlay forever.** (owner: game-dev, T9/T11; D4)
`removeStatus` correctly emits no `status:expired` (the contract says expired = ran out).
BurningOverlayView (src/game/views/BurningOverlayView.ts:26-49) removes the flame only on
`status:expired`, `unit:died` or unit removal. An origin that survives a Combust has no Burning in
core, but the view would keep drawing it, and no expiry will ever arrive to clear it. That's a
false "this one's cooked" read (instant read and GDD §7 rhythm), and a wrong one for the player:
the next spark won't Combust it. Fix without a contract change: BurningOverlayView also listens to
`reaction:triggered` and, for `reaction: 'combust'`, removes the origin's overlay. `consumed` is
only on the core result, and with `consumesStatus: true` every Combust consumes. If the
data-driven `consumesStatus` should matter to the view, T9 can pass it through. I check this in T9
and T11.

### Nit

**n1. `spreadTo` includes refreshes.** (owner: core-dev; producer if the event doc changes)
reactions.ts:205 builds `spreadTo` from every area status result, `'refreshed'` included. The
`reaction:triggered` doc in events.ts says "the units that caught Burning (ember arcs)". An arc to
an already-burning neighbour is harmless and arguably right, since its Burning was refreshed. Either
filter to `'applied'` or have the producer reword the doc to "caught or refreshed" next time
events.ts is opened. The `ReactionOutcome` comment already says "caught (or refreshed)".
