## Verdict: PASS WITH NOTES

Both modules are pure and take their tuning as parameters. The sweep is a proper ray against the
Minkowski circle (projectile radius + body radius), so no speed or frame rate tunnels: this is tested
for a spark against archer bodies at 30/60/144 fps and at the 100 ms clamp, at 38 positions each.
Team filtering gives exactly GDD §3: squad shots pass through wizards and kegs, arrows pass through
goblins and kegs, dead bodies are never hit. The projectile never looks at its shooter again, so a
dead shooter's bolt still lands, credited to the shooter. Knockback is ease-out quad with an exact
total at any frame rate and a tested per-frame bound. Nits only.

**Tie-break reading (core-dev asked):** I agree. GDD §3 says "the first enemy unit or hut they
overlap", which is first along the path. GDD §0's ascending-id rule is the tie-break for things that
resolve at the same moment, which here means the same contact distance. Picking the lowest id among
every overlap in the step regardless of distance would let a bolt hit a goblin *behind* the one it
reached first. The implementation and the test at projectile.test.ts:112 ("hits the first body
along the path, not the lowest id") are the right reading. No producer decision needed; M4.md's T6
wording is just loose.

### Nit

**n1. Arrow expiry distance is an unmade design choice.** (owner: game-designer, GDD text)
`createProjectile` takes `rangeFactor` from the caller and the module doc says "(GDD §3: 1.2)" for
every projectile. GDD §3 states 1.2 for *auto-attacks*; §12 gives arrows no expiry (see M4-T1
notes). game-dev will pass `COMBAT.projectileRangeFactor` for arrows too (264 px), which is sensible.
Add one line to GDD §12 Archer ("arrows also expire at `range × combat.projectileRangeFactor`") so
it's a design decision, not an accident.

**n2. `contactAlong` returns contact 0 for a body that already overlaps at the start of the step.**
(owner: core-dev, information only)
Correct and tested (:123), so a goblin that walks *into* a resting projectile is hit at once. One
consequence for T10: a projectile spawned at the shooter's centre while an enemy overlaps that point
hits on its first step. With the M4 bodies (bolt reach 16 vs grunt melee distance 22–28) it can't
happen, but huts are bigger (M7). No action.
